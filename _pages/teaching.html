What is Tri-Alpha?

As a first-generation honor society, Tri-Alpha creates a space that brings together the brightest first-generation minds. Here, we foster a strong support network, encourage scholarship and leadership, and provide a community for like-minded folk.

Why I started a chapter at AU

Coming to American University, I knew that the odds were stacked against me. I had no experience with higher academia; my only support came from my disabled, immigrant mother; and I had no safety net to fall back on. Only through self-sufficiency, hard work, and building up my credentials would I be able to succeed…or so I thought.

During my undergraduate degree, I was doing everything: TA’ing 3-4 courses a semester, completing research assistant work, completing a summer internship, networking with professors and peers, and soon began to run my school's behavioral research lab. Yet somehow, I wasn’t finding success in building my professional network, and didn’t seem to permeate the “academia-to-corporate” membrane everyone else around me was succeeding in. In searching for answers to what I was doing wrong, three distinct occurrences left a heavy impact on me:

  1. A mutual who was on academic probation and had never held a job got a paid internship in the Supreme Court building after their parents “talked” to some people.
  2. A mutual who attended a private high school in NYC mentioned their classmate got the fabled $100/hr Goldman Sachs internship right out of high school. When asked if this person was a prodigy of some sort, the mutual laughed and said quite the opposite, noting the family had some sort of connection to the Sachs corporate board.
  3. While visiting a mutual over break, we went to their country club. More than once, older members came up to offer her paid internships or full-time positions.

These occurrences were amongst dozens of events that began to illuminate the fact that we live in a largely cronyist culture—the job you get or the promotion you receive is based predominantly on your connections, and in-group positioning. Although these people weren’t doing anything wrong for accepting these opportunities per se, it’s quite clear that our culture has a hefty caste system, and if you want in, you better hope someone holds their hand down for you.

Although I’m a very stubborn individual, I’m not a revolutionary destined to lead the United States into a meritocratic state. I also knew that a hand would eventually most likely come down to rescue me from above. However, this wouldn’t fix the system—some of the smartest, hard-working trailblazers of our time would continue to face the same challenges as I have. After some time, I soon asked “why not bring the table to the table?”

As a result, under my advisor, Quintinella Merriweather, I helped establish Tri-Alpha at American University.
